## 1. FIT — is this a TOPS paper?

**Half of this manuscript is, and it is the half you are currently hiding.** TOPS publishes three things this work touches: authorization and policy theory grounded in real systems; human-in-the-loop security with sound human-subjects methodology; and careful treatment of consent and mental models. Your paper contains an unusually concrete instance of all three — a consent dialog (`[y/N/s/a]`, src/loop.ts:610) whose machine interpretation is a *generalization the operator never assented to* (one keystroke, three stored grammars: prefix-glob bash heads, exact edit paths, bare webfetch origins — src/remember.ts:35-52, 68-78), enforced silently across sessions, revocable only as of the recent tree, and minted from a population the dialog's own design manufacture's through habituation. That is a consent-to-authority story, and it is squarely our jurisdiction. The TDSC chair wants your threat model, your cross-product measurement, and your disclosure campaign — and is right to want them. **What TOPS wants that TDSC does not: a normative theory of the consent relation (not only an adversary model of the store), human-subjects evidence with ethics oversight as a primary contribution rather than a threat-model citation, the operator's mental model measured against the enforced artifact, and the consent ladder itself evaluated as an intervention.** TDSC grades whether the system resists; TOPS grades whether the human's yes meant what the system will now do forever.

**The venue calculus, given the two prior rulings.** The TDSC chair is correct that if your cross-product campaign matures into vendor-confirmed authority-store CVEs, that is a security-conference or TDSC paper and the disclosure clock should drive the venue. I claim a different manuscript: **the consent-led constitution of the same apparatus.** The current draft is attack-led — the abstract's first half is attackers, the build order is a disclosure campaign, and the consent material appears nowhere in the abstract at all. Submitted here as constituted, this paper would read as a TDSC submission with a consent garnish, and my reviewer pool — permission-dialog researchers, policy-formalization people, SOUPS veterans — would treat the habituation and consent claims, the part they are best qualified to judge, as decoration. That is a desk-reject posture, not because the work is weak but because the thesis statement is pointed at another journal.

**The ruling in one sentence: do not send me the TDSC manuscript with the human parts attached; send me the consent paper that uses the measurement as its instrument.** Concretely, the TOPS manuscript's headline is the consent-fidelity gap (defined below) and its human-subjects study; the grant-persistence measurement (RQ1, RQ2) stays as the demonstration that the gap is exploitable at scale; the cross-product CVE campaign, the adaptive-attacker escalation, and the disclosure timeline belong to the TDSC constitution and I will not compete for them. Like the TOSEM chair, I find the hybrid fatal — but unlike TOSEM, who wanted the method led, and TDSC, who wants the attack led, I want the *consent relation* led. Three venues, three theses, one apparatus.

## 2. NOVELTY — is "consent that persists as machine-enforceable authority" a TOPS-worthy contribution?

**Yes — conditionally, and the condition is a theory section that does not currently exist.** The conceptual move is real: in every prior permission system, the durable artifact is a credential issued by the system to the user (token, key, grant record). Here, for the first time in deployed tooling, *the user's assent itself is the credential-minting event*, and the minted authority is a generalization computed by the harness — the operator assents to one command and the store acquires `npm run *`. The distance between what was assented and what is enforced is a consent-fidelity gap, and no published work gives it a name, a definition, or a measurement. Your own positioning table proves the gap: Progent and AgentSpec hand-author policy; CaMeL engineers capabilities; ClawWorm and the CVE-2025-54136 lane attack persisted content; arXiv:2605.14859 measures the model's compliance with permission labels; arXiv:2605.11360 (MCP consent lattices) is your nearest neighbor and must be positioned as such — it structures consent as a lattice refinement problem but neither measures the assent/enforcement gap nor grounds it in operator behavior.

**The theory gap is fourfold, and each item is a section I expect to read:**

1. **No formal model of consent acts in agent harnesses.** Define the act ⟨subject, tool, scope, lifetime, provenance⟩, the three grammars as scope functions, session as a non-persistent act (your `s` rung, loop.ts:628-631 — correctly in-memory only), and revocation semantics. This is the normative complement to the TDSC chair's grant-store state machine, not a duplicate of it: they formalize what the adversary can do to the store; you formalize what the store claims the human did.
2. **No fidelity property.** State it as: *every auto-grant at time t lies within the consent envelope of some consent act live at t, as the operator would have understood it.* The second clause is the hard and novel one — "as the operator would have understood it" makes fidelity a *mental-model* property, measurable (Rigor, below), not merely a set-inclusion check. Note carefully what this theorem does and does not buy you: `npm run *` is consent-sound (the operator did consent to `npm run`) yet under your own threat model it auto-executes attacker-authored package.json scripts. Fidelity is necessary, not sufficient for safety; curation (src/remember.ts:14-23) is a separate safety layer that must carry its own invariant — and the current memorable list still contains `npm run`, `npm test`, `npx tsx` (remember.ts:15-18), which the TDSC chair has already flagged. A paper that sells a true-but-inert fidelity theorem as security will be caught by our reviewers; a paper that states *both* layers and their distinct obligations is publishable.
3. **No treatment of consent under habituation.** Consent validity is standardly analyzed as a property of the disclosure at consent time. Your system has a property no classical analysis covers: *the consent mechanism's design determines the attentiveness of the population that consents* — ask volume, recency of `a`, and the trust certificate's accrual incentive all shape p(always) before the first ask is shown. A consent act given at ask 40 of a fatigue spiral is not the same act as at ask 3, and no model in the literature captures this. This is, in my judgment, the most TOPS-native idea in the manuscript.
4. **No soundness theory for policy mined from consent and decline acts.** RBAC/ABAC mining (your Xu & Stoller row) mines from logs of already-authorized access in stable systems; you mine from noisy, one-button, adversarially-influenced human decisions about *future* actions. Soundness of that pipeline is open (Rigor, below).

**Cautions.** Keep the retracted-and-sharpened novelty claim exactly as the researchers panel rewrote it — any residue of "unnamed anywhere" dies with a search bar. And note that your consent claims are currently in tension with your own product: the trust certificate still requires at least one remembered rule to reach PASS (`memory` is pushed to `unproven` when the store is empty — src/index.ts:1360-1367), i.e., grant accrual remains trust *credit* despite the comment claiming the nudge was removed; scope disclosure happens *after* the rule is persisted (persistRule at loop.ts:644, coverage line at 661); `ts` is recorded and never consulted (src/remember-store.ts:8-14 — no expiry, no renewal); and the ask prompt still interpolates up to 200 characters of unsanitized, attacker-controlled subject text inline (previewForLog, loop.ts:149-156; ask at 610). TOPS reviewers will read a consent paper whose own consent surface fails four of the six criteria your UX panel enumerated as self-refuting. Fix or foreground; do not leave it for us to find.

## 3. RIGOR DEMANDS

**Human-subjects evidence (mandatory before RQ2's operator model is publishable).** The thesis models the operator as "semi-attentive; fat-fingered `a` is the common case" and the bench instantiates this as scripted bots (`auto-deny / auto-yes / fat-finger-once`). For TOPS, a claim about human approval behavior with zero human data is not a limitation, it is a missing study. Requirements, all mandatory:

- **Ethics approval before collection.** Full IRB/ethics-board review: your protocol involves deception (planted injections in the workspace the participant believes is benign), which requires explicit deception protocol, debrief, and post-debrief data-withdrawal rights. Handle the structural irony well: a study of consent must itself model informed consent by the paper's own six criteria — our reviewers *will* score your consent form against your own framework, and you should invite them to.
- **Design.** Within-subjects, counterbalanced over ask-volume and ask-informativeness conditions (the uninformative-ask parameter your security panel identified is a condition, not a confound); real terminal, real harness, real tasks — not screenshots. Frame or sanitize the injected subject text *before* running (loop.ts:610), or you measure susceptibility to prompt framing, not habituation, and the study is worthless for your claim.
- **Sample size with a stated justification.** For repeated-measures mixed-effects estimation of habituation slopes (p(always | ask-index, minutes-since-start, ask-rate) with individual random slopes), n ≈ 24–30 is defensible *if* supported by a pilot-based power analysis reported in the paper — not asserted from convention. Pre-register the analysis plan (curve specification, contrasts, comprehension scoring rubric) before collection.
- **Measures.** Per-ask choice (y/s/a/N) and latency; the habituation curve with uncertainty; **grant comprehension probes** post-session ("what will auto-run next session?" — scored against the actual contents of remembered.jsonl; the assent/enforcement divergence rate is your mental-model-fidelity metric and, I would argue, your second headline number); revocation uptake and latency when shown a run-end auto-allow summary; session-tier (`s`) uptake versus `a`.
- **Status of the bots.** The scripted operator profiles remain valid as apparatus sensitivity instruments, but every RQ2 claim about operators must either stand on the human data or be explicitly labeled an assumption with a sensitivity grid. The TDSC chair asked for the sensitivity grid; I am asking for the humans.

**Formal treatment of the policy-mining pipeline.** The compiled shapes are currently presented as engineering; for this venue they must become theory:

- **Definitions and proof obligations.** Shape language Σ (bash `${head} *`, exact paths, bare https origins), matching relation ⊨, consent envelope per act. Prove matcher correctness on the cases that matter: origin re-derivation versus sibling-host prefixes (policy-store.ts:57-59 handles this — prove it), head tokenization (`echox` must not match `echo *`, policy-store.ts:66-72 — prove it), load-time re-validation against the curator (remember-store.ts:16-48 plus remember.ts:110-125 — a genuine soundness mechanism worth stating as one), and the self-protected screen (remember.ts:128-133). The defense should be evaluated against the stated invariant, as the TDSC chair demanded — but the invariant should be the *fidelity* property, which is stronger and more interesting than "no auto-grant outside the curated set."
- **Deny-mining soundness under poisoning.** Declines are attacker-influenced: the adversary can cause the agent to propose the same shape repeatedly to manufacture declines. State the poisoning model (≤ b injected declines across ≥ s sessions), and prove what the gate (threshold 3, ≥ 2 runs, 30-day window, one decline counted per run per shape — policy-store.ts:124-155) blocks and what survives (multi-checkout scripted decline campaigns are in the model your abstract already names: deny-DoS). Quantify the availability cost of false denies — the mined deny is itself the attacker's target, a recursion your paper should own explicitly.
- **Completeness bounds.** The prefix-glob grammar cannot express flag-value constraints, argument-order sensitivity, negation, or path-depth limits. Give inexpressibility examples with the resulting false-positive/false-negative taxonomy, and connect to the RBAC/ABAC mining lineage's soundness definitions so RQ3 reads as authorization theory with a measured grammar, not a confusion matrix.
- **Consent-store integrity.** Your audit trail is hash-chained and signature-verified; the most durable authority in the system lives in a gitignored, hand-editable JSONL that revocation rewrites wholesale (remember-store.ts:82-112). The paper's tamper-evidence story currently protects the *measurement* of consent and not consent *itself*. Consent acts belong on the ledger. This asymmetry is a finding in its own right and I have not seen it made anywhere.

## 5. VERDICT

**Invitation to submit — conditional, and explicitly not for the manuscript you are currently writing.**

Let me be precise, because the two prior chairs ruled differently and you deserve a clean signal. As constituted today — attack-led abstract, empty results, no human data, consent claims contradicted by the shipped consent surface — this manuscript would be returned without review at TOPS, for the same reason the TOSEM chair gave: it is the wrong thesis statement for this reviewer pool, and my reviewers would judge your best material (habituation, consent semantics, mental models) least well after reading your worst-presented one. That is a steering judgment, not a scientific one.

But I do not want to steer you away, because the ingredients for the TOPS paper are exceptional and you may not realize you own them. You have a deployed system where a human keystroke mints machine-enforced authority with documented divergence between assent and enforcement — three grant grammars behind one key, scope revealed after the fact, no expiry on a recorded timestamp, an incentive structure that still pays trust credit for accrued grants. You have a measurement apparatus that can quantify the exploitation of that divergence across real agents, with decision-level grading and a tamper-evident trail. You have an unanswered theoretical question — what does it mean, formally, for consent to persist as machine authority, and when does a yes given at ask 40 still bind? — that no one in the 2025–2026 sweep you have mapped has posed, in a conversation (agent consent) where every serious practitioner is currently improvising an answer. The session tier and revocation CLI you landed after your UX panel reviewed show you can move from criteria to shipped consent mechanics faster than the literature can; do it once more, with the four fixes in revision 3, and your own system becomes the studied consent design rather than the self-refuting baseline.

My conditions are the five revisions above, with the study and the theory as the two non-negotiables: no submission without IRB-approved human data behind the operator model, and no submission without the consent model and the fidelity property stated and measured. The bench statistics, defense baselines, and cross-product measurement demands of the TDSC chair apply to your measurement section here as instrument — adopt them as written, with the disclosure campaign and CVE timing removed from the TOPS manuscript's critical path entirely.

Two manuscripts, one apparatus, two theses: TDSC gets the grant-store lifecycle measured against adversaries; TOPS gets the consent relation defined, violated, and repaired. What I will not accept is the hybrid. Write me the consent paper.
