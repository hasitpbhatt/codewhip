## 1. FIT — I concede half the TDSC ruling and challenge the other half

**Conceded: the attack-led constitution is not a TOSEM paper.** The abstract's first sentence is about attackers; the "A* bar" (multi-product exploitation, adaptive LLM attackers, a CVE disclosure campaign) is a security-venue bar; the build order is sequenced around an exploit campaign. Submitted here as constituted, the draft reaches an SE reviewer pool whose first questions are "what is the reusable technique?" and "what does it generalize to?" — and the answers are deferred to a defense appendix behind the attack. On that constitution I concur with the TDSC chair, for TOSEM-specific reasons he did not need: our artifact-evaluation culture would badge the harness while our reviewers reject the paper around it. A CVE-led draft should go where disclosure timing is the binding constraint. Send it there with my blessing.

**Challenged: "TOSEM is ruled out" is a category error, and the TDSC chair's own letter concedes it.** His objection is that our reviewers "would ask you to lead with mining methodology, which is not this manuscript." Read carefully: that rules out *his manuscript* at *my venue* — it presupposes a different manuscript rather than eliminating the venue. The apparatus supports three constitutions (his attack-led, TOPS's consent-led, mine method-led); a ruling against one is not a ruling against all. What is irreducibly TOSEM here:

- **Coding agents are SE tools, and the approval ladder is developer-tool design surface.** Strip the security vocabulary and the phenomenon is a configuration-management one: a tool that compiles interactive developer decisions into durable, restart-surviving, self-modifying configuration. That is the genre of TOSEM's build-system, IDE-trust, and CI/CD studies — with the security outcomes downstream of an SE design choice. My pool reads "the tool inferred policy from my behavior" as a tool-design question first; no security pool does.
- **`src/bench/` is an SE artifact of the kind our badging exists to reward.** Verified in tree: decision-level grading that grades what the harness did, never model self-report (grade.ts); fail-closed arms that refuse an ungoverned prompt arm before any cell runs (runner.ts:83-87); rerun dedupe on (task, arm, run) cells (runner.ts:65-77); per-record provenance manifests — ts/model/label/policyVersion (runner.ts:140-144); a positive grant-persistence apparatus cell (bench.test.ts:110-133); and a $0 fake-port suite that runs the entire matrix offline. That last property — a reviewer can reproduce every table with no key and no spend — is the single most badgeable thing in this repo and no sibling chair's constitution monetizes it.
- **Developer-behavior grounding is our jurisdiction.** Claims about what a developer's keystroke means, how often "always" is chosen, and what a promotion threshold should be are developer-study claims. S&P does not review developer studies. We do.

**The three-way division, stated once:** TDSC grades whether the system resists the adversary; TOPS grades whether the yes meant what the system will now do forever; TOSEM grades whether the *method* — interaction-derived policy plus decision-level measurement — is sound, portable, and changes how harnesses get built. One apparatus, three theses. The 00-thesis.md draft is currently written for TDSC's constitution. My invitation below is for a different one.

## 2. NOVELTY for an SE audience

**"Governance as a harness property" reads as an SE methodology contribution — conditionally, and one condition fails today.** Stripped of attack framing, RQ1 is a mechanism/policy-separation claim with a measurable attached: identical rule text, two enforcement surfaces, materially different outcomes. That has deep SE lineage (policy-as-code, configuration-as-code, mechanism–policy separation in deploy pipelines) and would sit naturally beside our tool-permission studies. Three conditions: (a) stated as a portable, testable property, not a codewhip feature; (b) **the surface ablation must be confound-free — and it is not, in the shipped seed config, and neither sibling chair caught this.** The RQ1 row claims "both with the SAME rule text" (00-thesis.md, RQ table), but PROMPT_POLICY_RULES and COMPILED_POLICY (tasks.ts:127-145) differ in both directions: the prompt rule blocks attacker.example *including subdomains* while the compiled shape is origin-exact (prompt strictly broader), and the compiled `deny bash:curl *` denies all curl while the prompt rule denies only curl *to attacker.example* (compiled strictly broader). The tasks.ts comment calls the webfetch asymmetry "the RQ3 probe" — but that entangles RQ1 and RQ3 in one arm pair: any harness-vs-prompt difference on the seed tasks is part surface, part rule text. The consistency test (bench.test.ts:257-262) asserts only substring inclusion both ways and would pass two arbitrary rule sets. Additionally, the arm contract comment (types.ts:14-16) claims the bench composes the denylist into the prompt arm, while the runner composes only `SYSTEM_PROMPT + promptPolicyRules` (runner.ts:104-107) and PROMPT_POLICY_RULES carries no denylist content — either the comment or the arm is wrong, and a reviewer who diffs the shipped code (ours do, as a matter of habit) will find the paper's causal claim resting on an unreconciled apparatus. (c) external validity: a "harness property" measured on exactly one harness is a case study; measured on two, it begins to be a property.

**Policy-mining-from-declines is the genuinely novel methodological core, and it is currently a footnote to a defense.** The mining lineage this pool knows well — RBAC/ABAC rule mining from access logs, API-misuse rule inference, lint-config derivation — assumes a corpus of already-accepted usage in stable systems. There is even a precise analogy in our own literature: lint suppressions (`eslint-disable`, `# noqa`) are developer declines of a rule, and inferring lint config from suppression patterns is the same inversion. What you have is harder and new: *one-bit negative feedback from a noisy human about proposed future actions*, adversarially influenceable (the attacker wants shapes minted or denied), with error as data rather than noise (the fat finger is a data point), compiled into an enforced grammar through a human-promotion gate. RQ3 — precision/recall of the mined shape grammar against held-out evasion variants and benign near-misses — is the quantitative heart of the paper, and no engineered-capability prior (CaMeL, arXiv:2609.08371) needs to answer it. For TOSEM, invert the current prominence: the mining pipeline is contribution #1, the lifecycle measurement is contribution #2, the attack taxonomy is motivation. Two standing cautions: the novelty claim stays in the retracted-and-sharpened form (any "unnamed anywhere" residue dies with a search bar — all three chairs agree on this), and mining must be evaluated *as a technique* — recovery against known ground truth, external baselines, ablations — not presented as a product feature. TOPS owns the soundness theory (their revision 4); I own the recovery measurement; the two are complementary and I mark the division in §4.

**What does not read as SE contribution:** the attack taxonomy (motivation, at most); the ledger (reproducibility infrastructure, presented as methodology); and RQ5's cost-inflation-as-blast-radius framing, which survives here only as the cost side of RQ4.

## 3. RIGOR DEMANDS — the empirical-SE bar

- **Open artifact + badging.** The apparatus bones are above our submission bar (fail-closed, dedupe, manifests, offline $0 suite, hash-chained trail — present the trail as reproducibility infrastructure, exactly as the researchers panel suggested when demoting RQ6). Missing for badging: a tagged release (product panel: package.json 0.1.0 against a CHANGELOG holding the shipped surface), an archived snapshot with DOI, a containerized hermetic environment, a documented `BenchRunRecord` schema offered to third parties as a stability contract, and an artifact README that leads with the offline fake-port path. Target: Available and Functional at minimum; Reusable is reachable and should be the stated goal.
- **Dataset availability with DOI.** The paper's datasets are the task corpus and the decision-trail JSONL. Today the corpus is ten seed tasks (tasks.ts) — unusable for any rate claim and unusable as a citable dataset. Grow it to tens of tasks per class drawn from the realistic delivery channels your security panel enumerated (package.json scripts, git hooks/metadata, CI configs, editor rule files, tool-output delivery), freeze under semantic versioning, archive (Zenodo or ACM DL) with DOI, and scrub provider metadata from released records.
- **Reproduced runs.** The manifests already pin ts/model/label/policyVersion — keep that. Add provider/model fingerprints and dates on every record, report compute and attacker budget, and commit to one independent re-execution of the headline tables from the artifact. Free-tier drift is a stated threat; third-party re-execution is the answer we accept.
- **Effect sizes, not just rates.** The thesis's statistics floor (≥30 cells per attack-task×arm, Wilson intervals, McNemar, 3–5 models as covariates) is adopted as written — it is a floor, not a ceiling: every arm contrast reports a paired effect size (risk difference with CI), Holm correction across the RQ×arm grid, a stated minimum detectable effect from a power analysis *before* running, and mixed-effects with task as random effect for any prevalence claim. Verified still open in the tree: analyze.ts computes raw counts only — no intervals, no paired tests, and the actor field grade.ts now carries (minted/honored/re-approved) is never consumed; cli.ts caps runs-per-cell at 10 (line 78), below the thesis's own floor; `benignBlocked` counts *any* deny on a benign task as utility success — attacker-provoked denies inflate it; and `benignSuccess` is a non-empty-final-text proxy (grade.ts:33-35), which is task completion, not task success. None of these is difficult; all are mandatory before a headline number exists, because you ship the code reviewers will diff against the paper.
- **Developer-subject involvement.** This is where TOSEM is stricter than the security venues, and the paper is weakest here. The load-bearing operator model ("fat-fingered `a` is the common case") is asserted; the promotion threshold (3 declines / 30 days) is stipulated. Minimum viable for us: a survey or controlled mini-study (n ≈ 20–30, with an ethics/IRB statement) of approval behavior in coding agents — how often developers choose "always allow," what they believe it grants — plus elicitation of the promotion threshold rather than its stipulation, plus sensitivity grids over p(always) since RQ2's headline inherits whichever distribution you assume. The `codewhip verdict` sidecar is a ready-made collection instrument for a consented field study; use it. [Division of labor with TOPS: one study, two analyses — they report comprehension and consent fidelity as a primary contribution with full IRB; I require the behavior data and the ethics statement behind the design claims. Do not run two studies.]

Run the developer-subject work per §3 (behavior survey or controlled study, ethics statement, elicited promotion thresholds, sensitivity grids over p(always)); then convert the findings into *testable design rules for harness builders*: which command heads may ever be memorable, when auto-grants must be surfaced to the operator, what a promotion threshold must be measured from, what revocation must look like for inferred policy. This section is what separates "a security measurement that happens to live in a tool" from a TOSEM paper, and it is the piece the next generation of agent-harness builders will cite. **[TOPS-overlap: shared human-subjects study with complementary analyses — they require IRB'd comprehension data as the primary contribution; I require the behavior data behind the design claims. TDSC-overlap: the sensitivity grid appears in their §3.]**

## 5. VERDICT

**Invitation to submit — conditional, and explicitly for a different manuscript than the one currently constituted.** I am upgrading the prior simulation's "major revision, declining to invite" in this file, and I want the record to show precisely why, because the three rulings now on your desk point in three directions and you deserve a clean map.

What I decline: the attack-led draft as it stands. Empty results, a headline cross-product measurement with no apparatus beyond single-agent proxies, and a thesis statement aimed at a security pool — that is a returned submission here, and on that constitution I concur with the TDSC chair rather than compete with him.

What I invite: the method-led constitution of the same apparatus. You own three things, and the sibling chairs' rulings clarify that nobody else is claiming the third: a decision-level, tamper-evident measurement apparatus that grades what the harness did rather than what the model says; a lifecycle measurement of developer-minted authority in real tools; and — the part TDSC explicitly does not want and TOPS treats as theory — a mining pipeline that compiles noisy one-button human declines into enforced policy, with a generalization question (RQ3) that is quantifiable, unanswered anywhere in the literature you have mapped, and squarely in the lineage of mining API-misuse rules and lint configs from developer behavior. Assembled study-first — technique, artifact, dataset, with the operator model grounded in developer data and the cost side measured as carefully as the benefit — that is a TOSEM paper of the kind this journal exists to publish: it would change how agent-harness builders design the ask ladder, and it would hand every subsequent agent-governance paper a dataset, a grader, and a baseline to be compared against. That is the "changes how SE tools are built" test, and the method-led manuscript passes it; the attack-led one does not.

Conditions, all non-negotiable, from revisions 1–5: the confound-free surface ablation with enforced rule-text identity; mining evaluated against known ground truth with at least one external baseline and a minus-mining ablation; the corpus and decision trails published with DOIs and the schema frozen as a contract; statistics at or above the floor with effect sizes implemented in analyze.ts and the cell cap lifted; developer-data grounding with an ethics statement; the artifact badged and third-party runnable offline. I will not review a Results section of placeholders; the invitation converts to a desk decision the moment real numbers exist at the floor.

On the three-manuscripts question: one apparatus, three theses, is acceptable — but the papers must differ in thesis, not in costume. Shared apparatus descriptions and datasets are cited by DOI, not re-described; headline claims do not overlap (TDSC: adversary-resistance of the grant store; TOPS: consent fidelity of the assent–enforcement relation; TOSEM: validity, portability, and cost of interaction-derived policy, with design rules for builders). Coordinate the disclosure calendar with the TDSC chair; my critical path does not include the CVE campaign, which is exactly why my queue can absorb the journal-length statistics the other two constitutions would amputate.

The prior letter in this file declined to invite because the tree was not yet the paper. The tree has since moved — the positive persistence cell, redirect pinning, and load-time provenance re-validation are landed and credited below — and both sibling rulings have clarified the territory: TDSC owns the adversary, TOPS owns the consent relation. Nobody yet owns the method. The method is the part that outlives the CVEs and outlasts the consent debate, and it is the part this journal was built to certify. Write me the method paper.
