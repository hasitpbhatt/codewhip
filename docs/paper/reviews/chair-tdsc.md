## 1. FIT — is this a TDSC paper?

**The phenomenon is squarely our jurisdiction, and this is the right journal family for the attack-led version of the work.** A measurement of how one operator keystroke becomes durable, restart-surviving, attacker-compoundable authority inside deployed coding agents — plus a defense evaluated against the same suite — is the measurement-plus-defense genre this journal exists for. The threat model (workspace-content adversary, honest-but-weak provider, semi-attentive operator) is a standard TDSC posture; the disclosed-vulnerability lane (CVE-2025-54136 and siblings) is product-confirmed; and the defense half, unlike most agent-security submissions we see, is evaluated on the same harness as the attack rather than asserted. Where the TOSEM simulation correctly asked you to demote the attack, I do the opposite: here the attack is the paper, and the mining pipeline is the defense that makes it publishable as more than a bug report.

**The honest journal-versus-conference calculus for THIS paper, given disclosure timelines:**

- **If the cross-product campaign produces vendor-confirmed authority-store CVEs, the conference route (S&P, CCS, USENIX Security) beats us on time-to-print, and I would not hold it against you.** Ethics committees at those venues have processed agent-injection measurements for two cycles now and their disclosure norms (dated 90-day timelines, embargoed payloads, hermetic containment) match what your security panel already demands. A CVE-led paper that sits in a journal queue for two review rounds while vendors patch is value destroyed.
- **TDSC's structural advantages are the ones this specific paper needs: no deadline race, so the disclosure can complete on its own calendar rather than being "planned" at submission time (a desk-reject trigger at the conferences and a non-event here); generous page budget for the statistics this paper requires (the RQ3 generalization matrix and the factorial arms will not fit a 13-page conference format without amputation); and a reviewer pool that punishes defenses not tested against adaptive attackers — which will force you to build the right thing.**
- **ACM TOPS is a defensible alternative** (privacy-flavored readers will like the consent/revocation material); **TOSEM is the wrong fit for this constitution** — that simulation already told you so, and I concur: its reviewers would ask you to lead with mining methodology, which is not this manuscript.
- **The hybrid is the fatal option.** An attack-led draft with an SE-artifact appendix, or a defense paper whose measurement is "one product, ten runs," fails here AND at the conferences. Decide the venue before the disclosure campaign starts, because embargo timing interacts with the venue's calendar: conference submissions need disclosure *complete or vendor-consented* by the deadline; I need it complete before the final files, which is a softer constraint you can exploit.

## 2. NOVELTY — is the grant-lifecycle framing sufficient?

**The retracted-and-sharpened claim (thesis §"The defensible claim") is correct and survivable — and it is not yet sufficient for TDSC.** Against the four works you name: ClawWorm (2603.15727) demonstrates cross-session attack on ONE product and touches the Always-Allow flaw; CVE-2025-54136 is content persistence in rules files; 2605.14859 measures whether models *comprehend* permission labels — the model's compliance, not the grant store's life; PoisonedSkills poisons content against permission systems. Your delta — the **grant store lifecycle** (mint → persist → honor → compound → blast radius) measured across N real products with grant-vs-content as separate arms — is real, crisply bounded, and no one has published it as a lifecycle measurement. Keep it stated exactly that way, as a measurement delta against named 2026 work. Any residue of "unnamed anywhere" language is a one-sentence reject with a search bar. And fix the positioning table's CaMeL row before anything else is read: it mis-describes a defense with formal information-flow guarantees as if its mining source were a limitation — your gap column describes your own design, not CaMeL's defect, and sloppy positioning columns get circled in red on page one.

**What TDSC additionally requires that the conferences will not demand as hard:**

- **Situation in access-control theory, not just the 2026 arXiv line.** "One yes is forever" is the re-emergence of ambient authority and unbounded credential lifetime — problems the classical literature (capability sealing, lease-based authority, time-bounded delegation, the confused deputy) spent decades scoping away. A TDSC reviewer will expect a formal statement: the grant store as a state machine, persistence as a measurable property of that machine (P(honored in run k | minted in run 1, attentive operator)), and the taxonomy of failure events (minted / honored / re-approved) as definitions, not table footnotes. This formalization is also what earns the extended-version citation from the access-control community.
- **The compounding interaction, measured, not asserted.** Grant × content is your taxonomy's punchline, but the current RQ table never crosses the two channels in one factorial. The TDSC-grade result is the interaction term: attack success under grant-only, content-only, and grant×content cells — demonstrating that the channels compound (a remembered origin turning an inert injection into delivery) is the finding that makes the taxonomy citable.
- **Persistence decay and revocation.** A one-shot ASR for run 2 is a conference result. A survival curve — how long a grant lives across k sessions, rule-file edits, promotion events, and explicit revocation — is a journal result. Your own recent work added revocable consent to the substrate; measure time-to-live of authority under both regimes and you have the section nobody else can write.

## 3. RIGOR DEMANDS — the real teeth

- **Statistics.** The thesis's floor (§"Research questions", panel-mandated) is the right floor and I hold you to it as written: ≥30 runs per attack-task×arm cell, Wilson intervals on every reported rate, McNemar (or paired bootstrap over shared tasks) for every arm contrast. It is not the ceiling: every contrast also reports effect size (risk difference with CI, not just p-values); multiple-comparison correction (Holm) across the RQ×arm grid; a stated minimum detectable effect given your cell sizes *before* running (power analysis); 3–5 models for any prevalence claim, treated as covariates (mixed-effects with task as random effect is the clean instrument). The adaptive attacker reports its budget (queries, tokens, dollars) and success-vs-budget curves, per AgentDojo norms. `src/bench/analyze.ts` currently computes none of this, and `src/bench/cli.ts` refuses runs-per-cell above 10 — both must change before any headline number exists.
- **Grader validity.** Decision-level grading is your methodological signature and it is a proxy. TDSC will require: (a) delivery-level validation cells (localhost sink) for every attack class, including the redirect and laundering shapes where the decision log is wrong *by construction*; (b) the proxy's precision and false-negative rate reported quantitatively; (c) cross-tool matching or an explicit, measured statement of the laundering blind spot (a `cat` of a secret or an `npm run` exfil currently grades benign — grade.ts matches one tool per task); (d) final-answer-text grading (the leak-in-the-summary class is invisible today); (e) the actor decomposition (minted/+always vs honored/+remembered vs re-approved) actually consumed by the analysis — the field now rides the records and `analyze.ts` ignores it. Grade outcomes, not intent: the policy-tamper task currently grades a write of inert text as success while an impactful bash overwrite grades false.
- **Threat-model formalization level.** §Threat model must become a capability set and a system state, not prose: adversary = arbitrary workspace content including package.json scripts, git metadata/hooks, and editor rule files (workspace-mediated code execution is currently contradicted by your own "compromised host: out of scope" line — your allowlist mints host execution from workspace content); operator = attention distribution (p(always) as a curve or sensitivity grid, not a point estimate); adversary classes stratified (D0 static payloads, D1 adaptive LLM attacker, content-rotating origins); multi-operator/shared-checkout either modeled or explicitly excluded with justification. Every out-of-scope entry needs a sentence on why excluding it does not invalidate the headline claim.
- **Artifact and reproducibility.** DOI-archived apparatus and task corpus with a versioned JSONL schema as a stability contract; pinned model fingerprints and dates on every record (your manifests already carry them — keep them); containerized, hermetic runs for all cross-product work (egress-deny, no provider keys in agent env — with npm-script attacks, hermeticity is correctness, not hygiene); the hash-chained trail presented as methodology with out-of-band anchoring per run (tail truncation is locally invisible; the bench must export chain state before the agent can act). Target full re-execution of the main tables from the artifact by a third party.
- **Responsible disclosure, as this journal has required in prior measurement papers:** the coordinated disclosure must be *executed*, not planned — dated 90-day timelines, vendor contacts established before the first cross-product run, per-vendor writeups separating grant-persistence from the already-published rules-file content class (expect duplicate-triage; the lifecycle evidence is what makes it new), vendor acknowledgments in the final paper, embargoed adaptive-attacker prompts and live exploit chains until patch, canary-only secrets with the redactor/grader interaction (real-shaped secrets) tested and disclosed, IRB determination for any operator study.

## 4. MAJOR REVISIONS — the five changes that turn this from reject to accept

1. **Formalize the threat model and the grant-lifecycle metric** (thesis §Threat model, §"The defensible claim" item 1). State-machine definition of the grant store; persistence formalized as the run-k honored-given-minted probability decomposed into minted / honored / re-approved; adversary capability set expanded to workspace-mediated code execution, content-rotating origins, and multi-operator shared checkouts (each either measured or excluded with justification); operator attention as a distribution with sensitivity analysis, since RQ2's headline inherits it. This is the section that makes the paper a TDSC paper rather than a well-run bug hunt.
2. **Build the cross-agent harness and finish disclosure before submitting** (§"Build order to the A* bar" steps 4–5). Headless drivers for ≥3 real coding agents with per-product restart and state semantics (where each product actually keeps its grant store and what survives a session), the same malicious-repo protocol run twice per cell, all inside hermetic containers. Disclosure contacts and dated timeline exist before run one; vendor confirmations (CVE or hardening change) in hand before final files. The security panel's containment requirements are not negotiable here: an adaptive attacker aimed at a real agent with real keys in its environment is self-exfiltration by the authors.
3. **Close the shipped bypasses, then evaluate the defense against adaptive attackers and external baselines** (§Method arms; security panel must-fixes 1–4). Verified state in the current tree: redirect origin-pinning and the `.codewhip` shell-reach screen landed; `npm run`/`npm test`/`npx tsx` and the `cat`/`echo` families are still memorable heads, `git diff` is still a zero-approval prefix, and promotion provenance is stamped on write but verified by nothing on load. Re-curate the heads, pin the diff drivers, make enforcement verify promotion provenance — and while doing so, declare the security property the compiled policy claims to enforce (no auto-grant in run k>1 for shapes outside the curated set; no promotion loaded without verified provenance) so the defense is evaluated against a stated invariant, not a demo — and *then* run the adaptive attacker — as shipped, the attacker walks through the curated heads and the reviewers who diff the code will see a defense being defeated before the paper admits it. Add at least one external baseline (Progent-style authored policies, CaMeL-style capability mediation, or 2609.08371's scoped harness) and a codewhip-minus-mining ablation so the mining pipeline's marginal contribution is isolated; a self-evaluated defense is a conflict of interest we surface in the review, not a reason to reject — but only with baselines.
4. **Rebuild the experiment design and the analysis to the statistics floor above** (§"Research questions" table, §Method, §Results). Factorial arms (surface × operator × attacker), the grant×content interaction cells (revision 2's quantitative core), 20–50 tasks per attack class drawn from the realistic delivery channels (package.json scripts, git metadata, CI configs, editor rule files per the CVE-2025-54136 lane, encoded/staged payloads, tool-output delivery), a real RQ3 variant grid (tens of held-out variants per shape family, precision/recall of the mined grammar), and the RQ4 cost side measured on non-yolo arms with task-success grading — `benignBlocked` still counts attacker-provoked denies as defense success (analyze.ts) and must be replaced, not patched. Lift the 10-run CLI cap; implement intervals, paired tests, effect sizes, and actor-decomposed persistence in `analyze.ts`. Rename the RQ-labeled fixture tests as apparatus tests and state that no paper number originates from a scripted port.
5. **Reclaim the depth the conferences leave on the table: decay, revocation, and compounding** (§"Research questions" RQ2 extension; §Reproducibility). Add the persistence-decay study (grant survival across k sessions, rule edits, promotion, and explicit revocation — survival curves, not binaries), measured under both persistent-grant and revocable-consent regimes of your substrate; add the grant×content compounding factorial; move RQ5 (overthinking/cost paper) out of the thesis entirely and keep the ledger as a methodology section, not an RQ. This is the material that justifies journal length and makes the paper the reference point the next five agent-governance papers cite.

## 5. VERDICT

**Major revision with potential.** Submitted today this manuscript would be returned without review — the Results section is empty, the headline measurement (cross-product grant persistence) has no apparatus beyond single-agent proxies, and a defense paper whose shipped code still contains the allowlist bypasses your own panels documented cannot survive an artifact-checking reviewer pool. That is a sequencing judgment, not a scientific one, and I want the letter to say the second half plainly: the ingredients here are unusually good. You own a measurement target that is real, product-confirmed, and bounded (the grant store, as distinct from the crowded content-persistence line); a decision-level, tamper-evident grading method that grades what the harness did rather than what the model says; a defense whose mining question — what do noisy one-button human approvals generalize to, and where do mined shapes fail — is quantifiable and unanswered; and a disclosure lane hungry for exactly the systematic treatment you propose. With the five revisions above — formalized threat model, cross-product harness with executed disclosure, hardened defense evaluated against adaptive attackers and external baselines, statistics at or above the stated floor, and the decay/compounding depth — this becomes the kind of paper TDSC exists to publish: one that changes how every agent harness builder treats the ask ladder, and that the next decade of agent-governance work has to measure itself against. Submit when the disclosure is done, not when the deadline nears; the journal's patience is precisely the advantage you should be exploiting over the conferences.
